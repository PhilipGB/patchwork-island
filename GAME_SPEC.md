# Game specification — Patchwork Island: First Playable

## 1. Experience and scope

**Pitch:** A happy storm has scattered the village's supplies. Help the residents by delivering parcels, repairing a bridge, driving a van, rebuilding a playground and decorating your own home. Every job visibly improves the world.

**Target players:** advanced 5-year-old through 10-year-old, sharing the same mechanics, with optional challenges for older players. **Target session:** 15–30 minutes to explore and complete several jobs; the full ten-job slice roughly 30–60 minutes depending on pace. **Feel:** LEGO-like interaction, Wobbly Life-style free-roaming chores, Minecraft-style simple construction, Fall Guys-style forgiving obstacle course, Mario Kart-style vehicle checkpoints and Sims-style home decoration.

**Core loop:** choose job at job board (or journal) → travel to marked location → interact with world and solve small real problem → earn coins/unlock something/change world → explore or choose another job. Free-roaming remains available between jobs. Players can replay obstacle course freely and customise the home after credits.

## 2. Hard MVP boundaries

**Required:** one contiguous island village, 5 identifiable buildings, river + repairable bridge, trees, roads, shop, workshop, player house, simple park, delivery van, checkpoint circuit, ten distinct missions, job board, HUD, pause/settings, basic sound feedback (optional if platform prevents), simple cosmetics/furniture, save/load, practical tests, Windows-targetable export.

**Not in MVP:** multiplayer, split-screen, AI traffic, physics ragdolls, building an unrestricted Minecraft-like voxel world, dialogue trees, quests generated by LLM, deep crafting/currency economy, a Sims-like needs simulation, boat, second island, weather cycle, multiplayer course sharing, combat, physics-intensive destruction, inaccessible interiors, monetisation.

## 3. World, spatial layout and landmarks

Build one outdoor world ~160m × 120m on a low-poly island. Use local coordinates in metres, X east and Z south. Layout is a **schematic**: use `planning/map.svg` for readable placement; adjust exact geometry to support traversability.

- Player house (-35, +22): front door, driveway, small customisable interior accessed by door (simple same-scene interior or separate small scene).
- Job board and town hall (-12, +14): missions and progress.
- Red House (-20, -22): obvious **RED** sign + red roof; early delivery destination.
- Market/shop (+4, +6): buy groceries with dedicated task purse; later sell house items for coins.
- Workshop (+24, -14): collect tools / receive bridge pieces; orange **WORKSHOP** sign.
- Resource grove (-35, -34) and stone patch (+18, -34): five wood and three stones, large interactive objects, clearly marked.
- Playground (-33, -3): two unassembled swings and an obvious worksite.
- River: runs north/south along approximately X=+45; keep village west of the river, park/race area east. Use stable collision and safe riverbanks. Falling in water triggers a friendly return to nearby ground, not failure.
- Bridge at (+45, 0): closed or damaged to start, becomes physically traversable on M03 completion.
- Meadow/obstacle and road course (+65, +10): four numbered checkpoint arches, a few wide ramps/steps, an obvious start and finish. Access after bridge repair. Provide a generous turning radius and no jumps that demand timing.
- Delivery van: parked near the player house, always recoverable via its parking sign or Reset Vehicle action.

**Important:** include obvious roads and sightlines, readable silhouette landmarks, a bright target marker, visible job stations and readable signs. No maze navigation required. Five buildings means player house, town hall, red house, shop, workshop; work sites are additional outdoor props, not buildings.

## 4. Controls and camera

| Context | Input | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Walking | WASD / arrows | Camera-relative move, with predictable gradual facing |
| Walking | Space | Forgiving jump (optional coyote time / buffered jump) |
| Walking | E | Interact with closest highlighted target in generous range (~3m) |
| Walking | Mouse wheel / right-drag | Optional zoom / orbit; auto-follow works without mouse |
| Driving | WASD / arrows | Accelerate, brake/reverse, steer van; simple arcade handling |
| Driving | E | Enter / exit (at low speed) |
| Driving | R or menu | Reset van to safe parking point (in driving mode only) |
| Building | Left click | Place selected snap-preview part on a valid ghost slot |
| Building | R | Rotate selected piece (building mode only) |
| UI | Left click, Tab/Enter/Esc | Select, activate, cancel, keyboard focus |
| Everywhere | Esc | Pause / cancel overlay; game always recoverable |

Avoid overloading a key in the **same** mode. Set visible context hints (`E: Talk`, `E: Enter van`, `Click: Place`, `R: Rotate`). Character should stand still while modal UI is open. Let mouse become visible for UI and building. A five-year-old must be able to complete deliveries with keyboard alone apart from clicking a job.

## 5. Camera and presentation

Default follow camera, slightly elevated and pulled back with no forced free-look. Target approx 6–8m from player, height 3–4m, angle down ~20–30 degrees. Smooth follow but no delayed camera motion that causes motion sickness. Prevent clipping into walls and minimise occlusion around buildings; optionally make blocked objects translucent. At 720p show job card upper-left, coins upper-right, hint/interact bottom-centre, simple compass/destination arrow; keep minimap optional or schematic (not a mandatory navigation system). Large text, large hit targets, high-contrast panels and pictograms plus words. Avoid reliance on colour alone.

## 6. Physics and object interaction

CharacterBody3D for player, stable floor and collision; allow small steps; auto-respawn to safe land from river. Use simple arcade vehicle controller, not an unstable rigidbody driving simulator. Pickups are interactive objects with a little bounce/rotation and counting feedback. A single generic interaction system can connect to dedicated gameplay systems (collect, deliver, buy, job board, open build site, furnish house). World feedback should happen immediately (object disappears into inventory, van loads a parcel icon, bridge gains real collision, swing visibly assembles).

## 7. Mission UX

**Exactly 10 missions, see `MISSIONS.json` for content and unlocks.** Player can have one active job plus available jobs in journal. Starting jobs M01 and M02 can be chosen in either order. A completed job stays completed. Job board highlights newly available jobs and offers a readable one-sentence instruction, progress counter and a single clear way-point. Keep mission completion success panels under 4 seconds or dismissible. Completing jobs earns coins and may unlock an object, area or cosmetic. Bonus time goals are always optional.

**No softlocks:** task-specific shop budgets are separate from permanent player coins; enough quest items are supplied/respawn; the van can be reset; bridge pieces are not consumed on invalid placements; furniture can be moved; reloading cannot reset a physically built bridge while keeping M03 marked complete. If the player leaves a job, its progress persists unless explicitly restarted by choice.

## 8. Literacy and numeracy progression

- **M01 / M02:** colour words plus labels, counting to 8, small quantities, iconic objects (apple, wood, stone).
- **M03 / M04:** combining 4+3, shopping 2×3+4 and change from 12, matching material shapes.
- **M05 / M06:** reading ordinal numbers and two addresses, sequencing 1→4, small multi-stop delivery planning.
- **M07 / M08:** understanding two-part assembly (2 swings need 4 ropes and 2 seats), comparing candidate objects against a budget of 20.
- **M09 / M10:** optional efficiency/route optimisation; home order 8+5+5+3=21 and furniture arrangement.

Never force the child to type an answer to progress. In-game actions prove understanding (picking up the correct quantities, buying requested goods, placing items). Optional 'how many altogether?' text prompts can be offered as bonus challenges; numerical keyboard entry must not be mandatory. Hints are layered: (1) reminder, (2) pictogram and highlighted item, (3) walkable breadcrumb/destination. A child who already knows the answer can act immediately, without clicking through lessons.

## 9. Rewards and economy

Mission coins: 10–20 per job (defined in JSON). Start with 0 coins; first easy jobs provide a reward. Coins buy home furniture, a character cap or van recolour. The **job shop** provides a separate budget for M04 and M08 so bad spending cannot make progression impossible. First job reward grants a red cap/unlock cosmetic; bridge reward opens east area; playground reward turns broken swings into an interactive play area; decorating reward shows decorations in world; finishing all jobs triggers village banner/confetti and replay suggestions.

Simplify economy: no loans, debts, starvation, delivery fines, breakable purchases or hidden prices. Display prices and remaining budget before selection. Undo/refund within each task is free. Players can always earn coins by repeating an optional activity or use a free starter furnishing set if insufficient funds.

## 10. Building and furnishing

M03 bridge building uses exactly 7 clearly marked ghost sockets: 3 supports and 4 planks. Supports go first, then planks. Clicking a correctly typed piece snaps and confirms it. Any wrong part stays available and receives a friendly hint. After all seven pieces are placed, bridge mesh/colliders switch to completed state, opening access to the meadow. Pieces are large enough to click easily. No fiddly rotation needed for bridge if sockets are pre-oriented.

M07 swings are assembled with 4 ropes and 2 seats across two frames. M08 place a bench (6 coins), flowerbed (4), lamp (9) inside the town green, using a **separate 20-coin job budget** and a reversible placement system. M10 house furnishing must support at least one table (8), two chairs (5 each), one plant (3), with grid snapped placements. Interiors can be tiny but must function visually and persist across restarts. A child may reposition items.

## 11. Audio and accessibility

- Provide short positive feedback for pickup, correct placement, completed job and purchases. If sounds cannot be produced, ensure all feedback also appears visually.
- Text in UK English, short sentences, common vocabulary and generous line spacing. Label both **RED** and the red destination (not colour alone).
- No essential time limit. Failures are reversible. Pause at any moment. Disable camera bob and flashing. Reduced motion / volume / text size settings if practical; at minimum music and SFX toggles.
- No obligation to read 100-word dialogue. Mission instructions should fit within 1–2 short sentences, broken into steps.

## 12. Persistence

A versioned save under `user://patchwork_save_v1.json` (or equivalent) contains: schemaVersion; completed/active mission IDs and mission-specific progress; unlocked gates/content/cosmetics; wallet; inventory; placed bridge, swing, park and house objects (identifier + transform); player and van transforms; user settings. Auto-save after meaningful events and on graceful exit. On invalid data, make a safety backup if possible and start with defaults, never hard-crash. Include a **New Game** path with confirmation and an explicit continue path.

## 13. Minimum quality bar

World has a coherent flat-shaded toy aesthetic. Character and van are distinct silhouettes. On opening, player immediately sees a job board marker and an easy welcome task. Every mission has objective marker, visible progress, a real successful completion state, reward and no unexplained dead-end. UI never blocks essential controls or traps cursor. Save/reload retains bridge, course unlock and home placements. All critical gameplay systems compile and boot under the pinned editor.
